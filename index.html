The case, in numbers

Four systems in production

Measured 2026-08-10 → 2026-09-17 · interventions 2026-09-04 → 2026-09-22 · helpdesk pulled 2026-09-22

1,368 students are learning across 42 different apps, and each app only knows its own corner. These four systems are what turns that into something a person can act on: one place that measures, a bot that answers, a log that closes the loop, and a library that stops the same work being done twice. This page states what each one is for, and puts the numbers underneath it.

The four systems

Measure

TimeBack Dashboard

Why it has to exist

A student works in up to 42 apps, each with its own console and its own idea of what accuracy means. Without one place that reads all of them, “is this child actually learning?” is a question that takes a person a day of tab-switching to answer — per child. At 1,368 students that question simply does not get asked.

The production platform every Campus DRI monitors through: XP, time, accuracy, tests and grade-level progress, per student per subject, with role-based guide and admin views, daily alerts, doom-loop detection and generated reports.

Answer

The Deep Dive Bot

Why it has to exist

The dashboard answers questions you already know to ask, in a browser, at a desk. Real questions arrive in Slack, from a guide, mid-lesson: “why is this student stuck?” The demand is measured, not assumed — in the same 18 days, 42 deep dives were requested by hand for 29 students, and the hand-built path closed 1 of them. 35 are still open, the oldest waiting 10 days. That queue is the argument for the bot.

A Slack bot that turns a plain-English question into a rendered report in the thread. The model picks one tool and writes a single framing sentence — it never restates a figure, because the tool result is rendered directly. That division is deliberate: a number cannot drift between the API and the channel, and an uncertain request comes back as a clarifying question instead of a confident answer about the wrong student.

Close the loop

The Intervention Log

Why it has to exist

Measuring a stall and doing nothing about it is just a report. When TimeBack does not resolve a student's stall by itself somebody has to intervene — and before this there was no record of what was tried, by whom, or whether it worked. It is the only record of remediation the platform did not do by itself, and the only place the trigger for each one is written down.

Two independent logs: requests a Campus DRI raises and pushes to a Curriculum DRI, and work the Campus DRI absorbs themselves. In 18 days it captured 613 campus touches over 355 students, with a median close of 17.2 hours.

A row is a touch, not a failure — the same student can appear more than once, so both the touch count and the folded case count are shown. The two logs are independent and are never totalled together.

Reuse

The Resource Library

Why it has to exist

The same study guide gets rebuilt at five campuses by five people who do not know the other four exist. Every hour spent regenerating an artefact that already exists is an hour not spent on a student who is stuck.

A shared library on its own Railway service with Postgres behind it, so a resource added by anyone appears for everyone rather than living in one person's browser. Two roles — staff see everything, students never receive staff-only rows — plus generated study-guide and practice-test pages.

Time to resolution — the distribution, not the median

The shape matters more than any single number: 24% of cases close inside an hour by automation and most of the rest take three days to two weeks, so the overall median falls in the empty valley between the two humps and describes almost nothing. For the 7,216 cases a human actually worked, the median is 6.7 days.

Median days to resolution, by ticket type

6.7 d → 17.2 h

A platform problem raised through the general helpdesk waits a median of 6.7 days for a human resolution. An academic stall raised through the purpose-built intervention loop is closed in 17.2 hours. These are different kinds of work and the comparison is not apples to apples — but it is the same organisation, the same period and largely the same students, and it is the clearest available evidence for what a dedicated loop buys over a general queue.

What this adds up to

One loop, built end to end

These are not four tools that happen to sit near each other. They are the four stages of a single loop, and each one exists because the stage before it produced something nobody could act on.

Method & provenance

  • Accuracy — one API call per student over the whole Alpha roster (2,037 resolved students, 1,368 with recorded activity in the window), 2026-08-10 to 2026-09-17, pulled 2026-09-18. Cohort key is the student's own OneRoster grade, not a per-subject working grade. Figures are pooled (Σ correct ÷ Σ answered), never an average of per-student averages. The 42 “apps” are the distinct source names the API reports, which includes a few internal surfaces (TimeBack Dash, manual XP assignment) alongside the learning apps proper; the by-app chart shows the 16 largest by volume, all of which are real apps.
  • Interventions — read from the alpha_dri_interventions source, scoped to its published rules: the two section values are independent logs and are never totalled; a row is a touch, so folded case counts are given alongside; minutes are read from minutes_spent for Campus DRI manual work and completion_minutes for Curriculum DRI answers, which the store keeps disjoint by constraint.
  • Turnaround — completed_at − created_at over completed Campus DRI rows. All 414 qualify. The 172 Curriculum DRI rows are excluded because that log is written retrospectively, which would otherwise produce negative elapsed times.
  • Support tickets — the Kayako instance carries 40,593 cases across ~219 brands and its API accepts no brand filter, so the pull reads every case and filters locally to the 14,403 Alpha-brand ones, of which 10,973 classify into a named type. Resolution is measured to last_completed_at, the agent's resolving reply — not last_closed_at, which is an auto-close firing days later and reads 72 hours against a real resolution of 0.1.
  • Repository figures — counted live from the working copies at build time with git shortlog and grep, not recalled.
